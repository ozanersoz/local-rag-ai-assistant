# South America Economy, Resources, and Trade

Source basis: paraphrased notes from Wikipedia pages about South America, Mercosur, Pacific Alliance, Amazon basin, lithium triangle, and country pages.

South American economies include agriculture, mining, energy, manufacturing, tourism, and services. Major exports vary by country and region. Brazil has a large diversified economy; Argentina is known for agriculture and industry; Chile is strongly associated with copper; Peru and Bolivia have important mining sectors; Colombia and Venezuela have significant oil-related histories.

The continent contains major natural resources, including petroleum, natural gas, copper, iron ore, lithium, gold, soybeans, coffee, beef, timber, and hydroelectric potential. Resource development is often connected to debates over environmental protection, Indigenous rights, infrastructure, and export dependency.

The lithium triangle is generally associated with salt flats in Argentina, Bolivia, and Chile. Lithium is important for rechargeable batteries and energy storage, making the region significant in discussions of electric vehicles and clean-energy supply chains.

Mercosur is a regional trade bloc associated with economic integration in South America. Its full members include Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay, with other countries connected through different membership or association arrangements over time.

The Pacific Alliance links several Pacific-facing Latin American economies. In South America, Chile, Colombia, and Peru are members, connecting them to trade networks around the Pacific.
