# South America History and Civilizations

Source basis: paraphrased notes from Wikipedia pages about South America, the Inca Empire, Spanish colonization of the Americas, Portuguese colonization of the Americas, and related country pages.

South America has a long human history before European colonization. Indigenous societies developed many different languages, agricultural systems, trade routes, and political structures across the Andes, Amazon Basin, Caribbean coast, pampas, and southern regions.

The Inca Empire was one of the most important pre-Columbian civilizations in western South America. It was centered in the Andes and connected large areas through roads, administrative centers, agriculture, and tribute systems. Its influence is especially associated with present-day Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Chile, Argentina, and Colombia.

European colonization transformed the continent after Spanish and Portuguese expansion. Spain controlled much of western and northern South America, while Portugal colonized Brazil. The colonial period changed political borders, languages, economies, religions, and demographics.

Independence movements in the nineteenth century produced the modern countries of South America. Spanish-speaking republics emerged across much of the continent, while Brazil became independent from Portugal. Later nation-building involved border conflicts, constitutional changes, immigration, export economies, and debates over federalism and central authority.
