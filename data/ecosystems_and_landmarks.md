# South America Ecosystems and Landmarks

Source basis: paraphrased notes from Wikipedia pages about the Amazon rainforest, Amazon basin, Andes, Pantanal, Atacama Desert, Lake Titicaca, Galápagos Islands, Patagonia, and Iguazu Falls.

The Amazon rainforest covers much of the Amazon Basin and extends across several South American countries. It is especially associated with Brazil, Peru, Colombia, Bolivia, Ecuador, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana, and nearby regions. It is one of the world's largest tropical rainforest systems and is known for biodiversity.

The Andes are the major mountain system along western South America. They run through or near Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina. The Andes influence climate, settlement, agriculture, mining, transportation, and cultural history.

The Pantanal is one of the world's largest tropical wetland systems. It is mostly in Brazil but also extends into Bolivia and Paraguay. It is important for wildlife, seasonal flooding, ranching, tourism, and conservation.

The Atacama Desert lies along the Pacific side of northern Chile and nearby regions. It is known as an extremely dry desert and is important for astronomy, mining, salt flats, and distinctive landscapes.

Lake Titicaca is a large high-altitude lake in the Andes on the border of Peru and Bolivia. It is culturally important and is associated with Indigenous history, transport, tourism, and highland geography.

Patagonia is a southern region associated mainly with Argentina and Chile. It includes mountains, glaciers, steppe landscapes, coastlines, national parks, and tourism destinations.

The Galápagos Islands belong to Ecuador and are located in the Pacific Ocean west of the South American mainland. They are famous for biodiversity and their role in the history of evolutionary science.

Iguazu Falls lies on the border region between Argentina and Brazil. It is one of the major waterfall systems of South America and is protected by national parks.
