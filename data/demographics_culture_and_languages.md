# South America Demographics, Culture, and Languages

Source basis: paraphrased notes from Wikipedia pages about South America, country pages, languages of South America, Quechuan languages, Guarani, and related culture sections.

Spanish is widely spoken across most of South America, including Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay, and Venezuela. Portuguese is the official language of Brazil, the continent's largest country by area and population.

Dutch is the official language of Suriname, while English is the official language of Guyana. French is used in French Guiana because it is an overseas department and region of France.

Indigenous languages are important across the continent. Quechua and Aymara are especially associated with Andean countries such as Peru and Bolivia. Guaraní is important in Paraguay, where it is widely associated with national identity alongside Spanish.

South American cultures reflect Indigenous, European, African, and immigrant influences. Music, food, literature, sports, religion, and festivals vary widely across the continent. Football is especially prominent across many countries.

Major urban centers include São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Buenos Aires, Bogotá, Lima, Santiago, Caracas, Quito, Guayaquil, Montevideo, Asunción, La Paz, Santa Cruz de la Sierra, and Medellín.
